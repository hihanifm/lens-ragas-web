Evaluating RAG
with RAGAS

Why measurement matters, what the metrics mean,
and how to run it directly from your browser.

The Problem

You can't tune what you can't measure

RAG has multiple failure modes

  • Retrieval fetches the wrong chunks
  • Retrieval misses relevant chunks entirely
  • Answer contradicts the retrieved context
  • Answer ignores the question entirely

Traditional metrics fall short

  • BLEU / ROUGE measure surface overlap, not meaning
  • Require exact reference strings
  • Give no signal on retrieval quality
  • Don't scale to long-form or technical answers
Consequence: without structured eval, every prompt or embedding change is a guess. You won't know if things got better or worse until users complain.

The Solution

What is RAGAS?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation Assessment

Open-source Python library by Exploding Gradients. Reference-free evaluation framework purpose-built for RAG pipelines.

LLM-as-Judge approach

Uses an LLM to assess whether claims in the answer are grounded in context, whether retrieved chunks are relevant, and more — without needing a human reviewer for every row.

Metrics

Four metrics, two concerns

Metric 1 of 4

Context Precision

Measures whether the retrieved chunks are actually useful for answering the question, judged against the ground truth. Penalises irrelevant chunks even if the right one is present.

CP = (relevant chunks retrieved) / (total chunks retrieved)

Example

Q: "Max torque of PN-4821?"
Retrieved: 3 chunks — 2 mention torque specs, 1 is about shipping dimensions
CP = 2/3 ≈ 0.67

High score (≥ 0.8)

Most retrieved chunks directly support the answer. Retrieval is precise.

Mid score (0.4 – 0.8)

Some noise. May improve by reducing k or tightening the reranker threshold.

Low score (< 0.4)

Retrieved chunks are mostly irrelevant. Embedding model or context column choice needs review.

Metric 2 of 4

Context Recall

Measures whether the retrieved chunks cover all the information needed to produce the ground truth answer. Detects gaps — relevant information that exists in the corpus but was not retrieved.

CR = (GT sentences supported by context) / (total GT sentences)

The LLM judge decomposes the ground truth into atomic claims and checks each against the retrieved context.

Example

Ground truth: "Max torque is 45 Nm. Operating temp is –20°C to 85°C."
Retrieved context covers torque but not temp range.
CR = 1/2 = 0.50

Precision vs Recall trade-off

Increasing k (more chunks) typically improves recall but hurts precision. RAGAS lets you measure both so you can find the sweet spot.

Low recall signals

  • k value is too small
  • Contextual content columns are missing key fields
  • Multi-part questions need broader retrieval
  • Embedding model underperforms for this domain

Metric 3 of 4

Faithfulness

Measures whether every claim in the generated answer is directly supported by the retrieved context. Detects hallucination — the LLM inventing facts not present in the chunks.

F = (claims supported by context) / (total claims in answer)

Example

Context: "PN-4821 max torque: 45 Nm."
Answer: "Max torque is 45 Nm. It was designed in Germany." (2 claims)
Germany is not in the context.
F = 1/2 = 0.50

Why it matters for LENS

LENS is search-only, so it has no generated answer. Faithfulness becomes relevant when you build a generation layer on top of LENS results — checking that the LLM stays within the retrieved content.

Requires the answer column

Supply a CSV with a generated answer column to unlock this metric in lens-ragas-web.

Metric 4 of 4

Answer Relevancy

Measures whether the generated answer actually addresses the question asked. An answer can be factually correct yet irrelevant — e.g. answering a different aspect of the question.

AR = avg cosine_sim(generated_questions, original_question)

The LLM judge reverse-engineers candidate questions from the answer, then measures embedding similarity to the original.

Example

Q: "What is the max torque?"
Answer: "The part number is PN-4821 and it ships in 3 days."
Technically true but completely off-topic.
AR ≈ 0.12

Faithfulness vs Relevancy

FaithfulRelevant
Hallucinated but on-topic✗✓
Grounded but off-topic✓✗
Ideal answer✓✓

Requires answer column

Uses embedding similarity — also requires an embeddings model to be configured alongside the judge LLM.

Interpretation

Reading the scores

≥ 0.80

Good

Pipeline is performing well on this metric. Still worth monitoring for regressions.

0.50 – 0.79

Needs attention

Noticeable gaps. Investigate the low-scoring rows — they usually cluster around a specific topic or query type.

< 0.50

Investigate

Systematic issue. Review your context columns, k value, or embedding model choice.

Tip: scores only become meaningful when you compare runs. Establish a baseline, then re-run after each change to your ingestion, k value, or model. The delta matters more than the absolute number.

Our Lab

Using lens-ragas-web

1
Prepare your test set

A CSV with a question column and a ground_truth column. Even 20–30 representative questions gives useful signal.

2
Export from LENS

In LENS → Search → upload your test CSV → click Export RAGAS JSON. This runs retrieval for every question and bundles the results.

3
Open lens-ragas-web

Drop the JSON into the upload zone. The tool auto-detects the LENS format and enables context metrics.

4
Run & export

Choose your judge (Ollama on-prem or OpenAI), click Run Evaluation, watch scores stream in row by row. Export CSV when done.

What you get

  • Per-question scores you can cross-reference with LENS
  • Aggregate mean for each metric
  • Colour-coded table (green / amber / red)
  • CSV export for tracking over time
  • Run history saved in your browser

LENS format limitation

LENS is retrieval-only — no generated answer. Context Precision and Context Recall are the two actionable metrics from a standard LENS export. To use Faithfulness and Answer Relevancy, provide a CSV with an answer column from your generation layer.
